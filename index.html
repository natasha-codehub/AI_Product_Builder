How I work

From ambiguous ask to shipped clarity

Every case study below follows the same shape. It isn't a framework I adopted. It's the pattern that was already there once I looked back at how these decisions actually got made.

01
The surface ask
Start where the brief isn't clean. The stated problem is rarely the real one.
02
What made it hard
Name the obvious first-instinct answer, then find out why it would have been wrong.
03
The decision
The first-principles call, and the insight that reframed the problem.
04
The trade-off I owned
What I gave up, what I was wrong about initially, what I'd do differently.
05
The result
Measured, not assumed. Real numbers, including the ones I didn't expect.

See it in action → the invoice automation case study below: a rules-vs-AI extraction call, a 2% tolerance threshold negotiated with finance rather than set by engineering, and the blind spot that cost two iterations on vendor-facing error messages.

Product casebook

How I break down ambiguous problems

Teardowns, root-cause exercises, and guesstimates. Product thinking on other people's problems, kept short on purpose. It's a sample of how I reason, not a replacement for the real work above.

Teardown

How Amazon delivers 9B items in 24 hours

The real problem wasn't inefficient routes. Customer expectations were compounding faster than the system could keep up. AI was the last resort, after forecasting, regional planning, and human dispatch all hit a ceiling.

Teardown / Vision

Carbon-transparent eCommerce, a 2035 sketch

Products carry an AI-tagged carbon label, checkout suggests eco-smart shipping, and loyalty systems reward planet-positive behavior. A vision piece on what sustainable commerce infrastructure could look like.

Guesstimate

Estimating steps taken yesterday

Clarify, structure, estimate, sanity check. It's the same muscle I use when a stakeholder asks "how much effort will this take?" Structure the ambiguity, make assumptions explicit, and always land on a number.

Root Cause Analysis

A 20% drop in Uber's quarterly revenue

Revenue = trips × average value × take rate. Localize the drop by business line and geography first, then investigate demand, supply, and monetization separately before chasing a cause.

Root Cause Analysis

Netflix KPIs trending downward

Which metric actually dropped changes the entire investigation. Six cause buckets, from content quality to competitive substitution, ranked by what a data cut rules out first.
