01 · The Surface Ask

The sensors were already streaming the answer

B2B industrial gas partners had IoT sensors on their tanks streaming live readings, and they were still running out of gas. The data existed. The problem was that nobody was using it to make decisions until it was too late.

02 · What Made It Hard

The obvious fix, a threshold alert, was the wrong fix

The obvious first instinct was a threshold alert: set a trigger at 20% tank level, fire a delivery order. I almost went with that. The reason I didn't: 20% means something completely different at a manufacturing site running three shifts than at a smaller partner closed on weekends.

A static threshold would either send deliveries too early and blow up logistics costs, or miss sites with high consumption variance and still have runouts.

03 · The Decision

This was a forecasting problem, not an alerting problem

I spent three weeks in delivery operations data before writing a single spec. The pattern was clear: every runout happened when consumption had deviated from historical average during a period nobody was watching live readings. The real problem wasn't the data, it was that the delivery calendar was built on fixed schedules, not actual consumption patterns. Some sites used three times more gas in winter. Some spiked during peak business periods. Neither the calendar nor a static threshold knew that.

That told me exactly what the system needed to output: not "this tank is low," but "this specific site will hit empty in X days, given its pattern and lead time." One requirement ruled out the rules-based approach before engineering wrote a line of code.

04 · The Trade-off I Owned

Shipping the imperfect version instead of waiting for perfect data

Not every site had rich data. Some had two years of readings, others were recently onboarded. I shipped two versions simultaneously: data-rich sites got model-driven thresholds, sparse sites got conservative manual thresholds with a larger safety buffer and an earlier trigger. We'd over-order at sparse sites for a few months while accumulating data. The alternative was waiting for perfect coverage before shipping anything, and I chose shipping the imperfect version over waiting for the perfect one.

05 · The Result

Zero runouts, without waiting for perfect data

Impact
Zero unplanned runout incidents across all telemetry-enabled sites.

Partners never went offline, and the business retained the relationships because the system was invisible until it was needed, partners didn't get an alert, they just never ran out. Emergency delivery costs dropped because the model caught sites before they hit critical, and the delivery routing team had predictable schedules for the first time instead of reacting to emergency calls. The thing I'm most proud of: we got to zero runouts without waiting for perfect data. If we'd waited for full coverage, we'd have had runouts while we were waiting.
